How to make an eyeball jar

The eyes start out flat on the kitchen table. Once the paper opens against the curved glass, they seem to look back at you.

Here’s how I fit the printable wrap, finish the lid and place the specimen label on a DIY eyeball jar.

What you’ll need

  • A clean, dry, clear glass jar with a lid
  • The printable PDF, a printer and A4 or US Letter paper
  • Scissors, a ruler and a strip of scrap paper for measuring
  • Glue for the paper labels

Optional finishes include black paint for the lid and a cotton swab with a dab of petroleum jelly for a curved shoulder.

Choose a jar and print the wrap

Measure the straight inside height from the base to where the glass curves inward. Fit a strip of scrap paper around the inside to check the circumference. The smallest wrap measures 28 × 11 cm and works well in a jam or peanut butter jar. For taller mason canning jars, the largest design suits jars up to about 19 cm high.

Select the pages for your design, size and paper format. Start at Actual Size (100%) and check the print preview for clipped edges. Cut around the artwork and keep the matching extension piece handy.

Fit the eyes behind the glass

Roll the paper loosely with the eyes facing out, slip it through the opening and let it uncurl. Look through the front of the jar as you rotate the print. I like to bring one whole eye into view just above the spot where the label will sit.

Turn the join toward the back. A small gap is fine on an oddities shelf where you see the jar from one side. For a display you can walk around, slip the matching extension behind the ends to fill the gap.

Trim the height for a curved shoulder

In the close-up, you can see where the glass turns inward near the neck. If the top of your wrap follows that curve, the paper can bow away from the glass. Take it out and trim a thin strip from the top. Try it again. A slightly shorter wrap can sit flush against the straight wall of the jar.

To soften the clear band above the paper, lift the wrap out first. With a cotton swab, feather a tiny dab of petroleum jelly (Vaseline) over just that band on the inside of the glass. A trace of butter can give a similar haze. Keep it off the printed paper and leave the outside of the jar clean. Slip the wrap back in and check the effect from the front.

Give the lids a matching finish

My three lids started out in different colors. I took them off the jars and painted the outsides black. Once dry, they looked like pieces from the same apothecary cabinet. If you already like the color of your lid, move straight on to the labels.

Place the specimen jar labels

Stand the jar at shelf height and hold the “Preserved Human Eyeballs” label against the front. Slide it down until your favorite eyes are visible, then glue it in place. Center the smaller Old Salem label on the lid. For a jar on a low gothic wall shelf, you’ll see both labels at once.

Build a cabinet of curiosities

For eyeballs in a jar for Halloween, one jam jar makes a simple tabletop prop. To build a year-round apothecary display, use the bonus brain and mealworm wraps with the same witchy bottle labels. All three designs come in small, medium and large in one PDF.
