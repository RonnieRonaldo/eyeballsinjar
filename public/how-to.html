How to make an eyeball jar

Start with a clear glass jar and a paper wrap. The curve of the glass gives this faux wet specimen its depth.

Gather your printed sheets, a dry jar with a lid, scissors, a ruler and glue for the paper labels.

  1. Choose your jar and print size

    Measure the straight inside height, from the base to where the glass curves inward. A strip of scrap paper fitted around the inside helps you check the circumference. Compare it with the wrap before cutting. The smallest wrap is 28 cm wide by 11 cm high.

  2. Print and trim the wrap

    Select the pages for your design, wrap size and paper format (A4 or US Letter). Start at Actual Size (100%) and check the preview for clipped edges. Cut around the printed artwork and keep the matching extension piece handy.

  3. Fit the eyes behind the glass

    Loosely roll the paper with the eyes facing outward. Slip it through the opening and let it uncurl against the glass. If an edge buckles, lift the wrap out and trim a little at a time. If the ends leave a gap, overlap the matching extension behind them. Turn the join toward the back.

  4. Give mismatched lids a black finish

    My three jars started out with three different lids. I took them off the jars and spray-painted the outsides black. Once the paint was dry, the jars looked like a set, even before I added the labels. If you like your lid as it is, skip straight to the labels.

  5. Place the specimen labels

    Set the jar on its shelf and check which eyes you can see from the front. Hold the label against the glass first, then glue it in place. Finish with the Old Salem Apothecary label on the lid or use it as your front label.
